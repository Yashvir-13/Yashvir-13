### 01. Perception & Representation

I write software to study perception, representation, and the boundary between human and machine interpretation. My work sits where physical reality—light, motion, gesture, and language—is translated into mathematical structure. Rather than treating neural architectures as opaque black boxes, I care about the geometry of the latent spaces inside them, and how classical constraints can keep modern systems honest. Outside of code, that same curiosity pulls me toward filmmaking, music, poetry, and philosophy: different ways of framing time, silence, and human attention.

- **Focus:** Intelligent Systems · Computer Vision · Representation Geometry
- **Current Inquiry:** Classical physical & epistemic constraints in modern neural architectures
- **Core Stack:** Python · PyTorch · C/C++ · OpenCV · TypeScript

### 04. After Hours — Cinema, Music, Poetry & Philosophy

When I step away from the terminal, I spend my time behind a lens capturing still and moving images, listening to and studying music, and writing essays and poetry on perception and human agency.

- **Cinema & Direction** — *Light, Frame & Temporal Pacing*: Filmmaking and computer vision ask the exact same first question: what happens when three-dimensional light is projected onto a two-dimensional plane over time? Studying optical flow made me a more attentive editor; cutting film taught me what motion vectors miss.
- **Music & Acoustics** — *Harmonic Structure & Resonance*: Music is geometry you can feel before you can name it. Whether analyzing Fourier transforms in signal processing or listening to a sustained chord resolve, the tension between mathematical ratio and human emotion stays constant.
- **Poetry & Essays** — *Precision Without Verbosity*: Good poetry and good code share an economy of language. Every word or line of logic should carry weight. I write essays and verse to slow down thoughts that move too quickly in a terminal window.
- **Philosophy of Mind** — *The Boundary of What Machines Know*: Wittgenstein wrote that the limits of my language mean the limits of my world. In machine learning, the architecture and loss function define what a model can perceive—and what remains outside its epistemic boundary.
